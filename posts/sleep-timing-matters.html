Sleep

Bedtime matters, not just sleep length

Getting enough hours is important, but the time you go to bed can matter just as much — and for some people, even more.

Sleep length tells you how much rest you got. Bedtime tells you whether your body had a predictable rhythm. A consistent sleep window helps your brain and body know when to wind down, when to repair, and when to wake up with steadier energy.

If you sleep eight hours but go to bed at very different times each night, your body may still feel like it is catching up. Late nights can also make it harder to get deep, regular sleep, even when the total number of hours looks fine.

Try tonight

Choose a realistic bedtime cue and start winding down 30 minutes before it.

A simple way to use this

  • Pick a realistic bedtime target — not a perfect one.
  • Protect the 30 minutes before that time as a wind-down window.
  • Track both bedtime and sleep length for a week.
  • If you feel tired, look first for big bedtime swings, not only short sleep.

For Health Tracker, the goal is not to make every night perfect. The goal is to notice whether your sleep rhythm is becoming easier to repeat.
